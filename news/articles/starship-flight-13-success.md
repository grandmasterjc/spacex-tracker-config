# Third time's the charm: Flight 13 delivers the first Starlink V3 satellites

**Category:** Starship  
**Published:** July 25, 2026  
**Hero image:** starship-f13-liftoff.jpg  
**Image credit:** SpaceX  

## Lead

After an engine abort at T-0, a weather scrub, and a last-minute hold, Starship Flight 13 finally left the pad at Starbase on Thursday evening — and made the wait worth it. The world's largest rocket deployed the first 20 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites, relit an engine in space, and brought its Ship upper stage to the gentlest splashdown in program history. Only the booster had a rough day.

## The hard road to liftoff

Few flights have earned their launch quite like this one. On July 16, four Raptor engines failed to ignite in the final seconds, triggering the program's first last-second abort and forcing an engine swap. A second attempt on July 23 was scrubbed for weather nearly three hours before launch. On Thursday, a hold in the countdown pushed T-0 back once more before the 33 engines of Super Heavy finally lit — all of them, this time.

## Twenty satellites that change the game

The headline achievement came about half an hour in: Ship deployed 20 Starlink Version 3 satellites — large, flat spacecraft with laser communication links and expansive solar arrays — and the Starlink team confirmed contact with all twenty. It is the first operational payload of the V3 era and a decisive step toward Starship taking over Starlink deployment from Falcon 9, which currently carries the entire constellation on its back.

## The softest splashdown ever

Ship kept delivering after deployment. It successfully relit a Raptor in space — the maneuver that underpins every future deorbit burn — and then descended to what SpaceX called the softest splashdown the program has ever achieved, settling gently into the Indian Ocean an hour and five minutes after launch. For the first time, the vehicle remained fully intact after splashdown, floating on the surface with cameras still streaming.

## The booster's hard landing

Super Heavy's day ended less gracefully. During the landing burn, eight of the thirteen planned engines failed to restart, and with only five delivering thrust the booster hit the Gulf waters hard. The splashdown was controlled, and expendable-mode data is valuable — but engine restart reliability on Raptor 3 is now clearly the program's number one open question, echoing the ignition problems that caused the July 16 abort.

## What it means

Flight 13 goes in the books as the strongest flight of the V3 era: payload deployed, in-space relight proven, Ship recovered intact. Solve the booster's restart issue, and the path opens toward catch attempts, higher cadence, and the Florida pads now nearing completion. The lunar program is watching too — every one of these milestones feeds directly into the Starship NASA is counting on for Artemis.
